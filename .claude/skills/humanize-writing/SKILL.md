# Humanize Writing

Based on widely-documented patterns of AI-generated text ("AI tells"). The problem isn't any single word — it's that these words and structures cluster together and make writing feel synthetic. Cut them.

## Banned words (abstract verbs / inflated adjectives / filler nouns)

Never use: delve, leverage, utilize, harness, streamline, underscore, unlock, empower, robust, seamless, cutting-edge, innovative, pivotal, crucial, meticulous(ly), intricate/intricacies, interplay, boasts, bolstered, garner, emphasizing, enduring, vibrant, valuable, tapestry, landscape, realm, synergy, testament, underpinnings.

## Banned transitions and openers

- Formal transitions: moreover, furthermore, consequently, additionally.
- Throat-clearing openers: "In today's fast-paced world...", "In the ever-evolving landscape of...", "Let's dive in."
- Summary closers: "In conclusion," "At the end of the day," "Ultimately," "In essence," "It's worth noting," "It's important to note."
- Email-specific clichés: "I hope this email finds you well," "I wanted to reach out," "Just circling back."

## Banned structures

- The AI negation pattern: "It's not just X, it's Y" / "That's not X, that's Y" / "Not by doing X, but by doing Y." Say the thing directly instead.
- Tricolon overload: three-part lists/phrases stacked for rhetorical effect ("faster, smarter, better"). Real people rarely reach for three parallel adjectives in a row.
- Perfectly symmetric paragraphs (every paragraph the same length, same structure). Vary it.
- Em dash overuse. Use a period, comma, or parenthetical instead most of the time.

## What real human writing looks like instead

- Concrete, specific details over vague abstractions — name the actual thing you noticed, not a generic compliment.
- Varied sentence length: a short sentence next to a longer one. Not uniform.
- Contractions (I'd, you're, it's, don't).
- Occasional sentence fragments or a slightly run-on sentence — real people don't edit themselves into perfect grammar.
- Ends with an actual next step or a specific question, not a recap of what was just said.
- Opinions and mild imperfection (a parenthetical aside, a qualifier like "honestly" or "kind of") over polished neutrality.

## Quick self-check before sending

Read the draft back and cut/rewrite anything that:
1. Could be swapped into a completely different email about a different company without changing a word — that's a sign it's generic template filler, not observation.
2. Uses any word from the banned list above.
3. Has three sentences in a row of similar length and structure.
